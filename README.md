# Analysis of Correlates of School Performance
This project uses data from the California Department of Education dataset that includes information on academic performance outcomes and a range of possible predictors for elementary school children in the state. 
Description: The importance of primary education in the development of children cannot be overstated. It is during this critical period that children acquire the foundational skills that prepare them for lifelong learning, work, and active citizenship. Quality education is essential for empowering children and young people, safeguarding their health and well-being, and breaking cycles of poverty. By examining academic performance data in context of other factors such as socio-economic status, race, gender, emotional drivers of education, and parental background, policymakers can identify the root causes of underperformance in academia and develop evidence-based interventions to address these issues. This project uses data from the California Department of Education dataset that includes information on academic performance outcomes and a range of possible predictors for elementary school children in the state. The dataset comprises school outcome measures for 400 elementary schools that were randomly selected from 37 districts across the state of California.

Summary of analysis results: 
The final model of this study indicates that at least 85% of the variation in academic performance of elementary school students can be explained by contextual variables, such as the socio-economic position of their environment, parental education background, quality of teachers, learning environment, and students' language background. To gain further insight into the relationship between socio-economic status and academic performance, additional analysis was conducted to identify school districts with high concentrations of students from low-income families. Using R, we grouped districts together based on the average percentage of students receiving free meals and identified the districts with the highest number of schools categorized as having high percentages of free meals (mealcat = 3). Districts 401, 253, 395, and 716 had the highest number of such schools. Furthermore, we also identified the districts with the highest average percentages of students receiving free meals (>80%), which were districts 253, 41, 716, 627, and 395.
